# Scenes

## Why you want to step into the hope pictures

The "magic" one doesn't notice consciously is made of concrete things. What we saw in the three scenes and the title:

**Why you want to step in**
- **A figure from behind in the middle of emptiness** — Caspar David Friedrich's Rückenfigur. No face, so the viewer
  stands in the hero's place: not "I look at a character" but "I stand there myself". Hence the wish to dream.
- **A distant warm light in a cold world** — a window among the firs, a firefly. A promise: you want to get there.
  The whole frame is cold, the warm point is one, and the eye is drawn to it.
- **Footprints in the snow** — a small story without words: someone has already walked this way.

**Why it feels soft and alive, not digital**
- Film grain over everything — you don't see it, but it removes sterility.
- A vignette: darker edges pull the eye to the centre.
- No pure black and no pure white — everything in a narrow range, as through haze.
- A halo around the moon, the lights, the fireflies: light spills rather than just shines.
- Fog in layers: each farther row of firs lighter and more transparent. Depth without 3D.
- Snow at three depths: large, fast and blurred in front; small and slow behind. The brain reads a volume of air.

**Why the motion breathes**
- Everything is slower than in life.
- Motion is not an even sine but has noise; nothing repeats in a visible cycle.
- The figure breathes — one or two percent of scale, nearly invisible, but without it the figure is dead.
- The moon's reflection is broken into trembling strips.

**Why the text whispers**
- Small serif italics, low contrast, two lines like verse. The menu is almost invisible.

**The title** ([original](pictures/hope/title.webp)) holds almost entirely on contrast of scale:
- the word "hope" takes about a fifth of the frame's height; the little figure with a lantern stands right under it,
  in the centre, about one letter tall — hope hangs over the child, huge, and the child is a point;
- the horizon curves like the edge of a small planet, and the figure stands on its top — alone in the whole world;
- more than nine tenths of the frame is dark sky, a band of fog and star dust — the emptiness works, it presses and
  draws you in;
- two light sources only: the cream word and the warm lantern; everything else in a narrow dark range;
- scale in the type too: a huge serif italic, and under it tiny spaced monospace, almost a whisper; the tail of "p"
  runs into the small word — the words interlace.

Pavel: these are particular cases. What matters is the general thing — a designer's conscious work, not rules taken
from these pictures.

## A mid-shot, if not avoided but made beautiful

Claude Design slightly cheated: its people are drawn silhouettes that sway; in moood people move, with motion made by
a model (Kimodo). And the documents push scenes towards the mid-shot (a speaker at least a quarter of the frame, not
everyone from behind) — exactly where the old games looked worst. So: how would an ideal mid-shot look, without
thinking of limits?

- **The person is designed, not assembled.** Each hero has their own silhouette, proportions, character of form —
  one angular and stooped, another soft and light; you know who it is from the shadow alone. Made of the same material
  as the world: the same softness of edges, texture, palette. Lives in it, not stuck on it.
- **Motion is acting, not a recording of motion.** A few strong poses chosen by meaning; between them the person
  doesn't freeze but breathes, shifts weight, turns the head a little; then a quick change to the next pose. Gesture
  slightly exaggerated, as in good animation. Every movement means something: she fiddles with her sleeve because
  she is awkward.
- **The face is simple but alive.** Eyes and brows carry almost everything: a glance aside, a blink at the right
  moment, a pause before an answer. Not a detailed face — an exact one.
- **Light shapes the person and speaks of the feeling.** Warm from one side, cold from the other, a glowing rim. Light
  drawn in shapes, as in "Klaus", not just computed. The background quieter; the strongest contrast on face and hands.
- **The camera as in cinema.** Off-centre, with air in the direction of the gaze; something out of focus in front;
  the background softly blurred; the camera barely breathes. And a mid-shot is never alone: it alternates with a
  close-up of hands, a reverse shot, a wide shot — meaning is born from how shots follow each other.
- **Secondary life.** Hair, a scarf, the hem of a coat follow motion and settle; breath in frost, dust in a beam, a
  candle flame in the eyes.
- **All of it one whole.** Grain, edges, colour, rhythm of motion — the same for the person, the world and the text.

Closest in spirit: Cartoon Saloon ("Wolfwalkers", "Song of the Sea"), "Klaus", Kentucky Route Zero.

The 2D try at this ([experiments/midshot](../experiments/midshot/)): Pavel liked the colours, the faces' expressions
and eyes, and many moments better than in moood; the bodies — the form of bodies and of their parts — were the main
fault, and the motion was super unnatural, algorithmically programmed. "And when these people start walking around
the room, it will be hell."

## What expensive games do that we don't

- **Nobody shows raw motion.** Even motion captured on an actor is then worked by animators: poses pushed, holds
  added, a hand adjusted for the camera, jitter removed. For us: a pass after Kimodo that pushes poses, holds pauses and
  fits motion to the shot.
- **Walking is matched, not played from clips** (motion matching: For Honor, The Last of Us Part II). A big library of
  steps, turns, stops and starts; each frame picks the piece that best continues the pose and leads where to go.
  Kimodo could generate such a library in advance.
- **3D that looks drawn is a craft of its own** (Arc System Works: Guilty Gear Xrd, Dragon Ball FighterZ): animation
  "on twos" — a pose holds two or three frames instead of floating (also "Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse"); face
  shadows edited by hand to fall beautifully; the model slightly deformed for the camera angle.
- **Small life is spring bones** — hair, skirts, scarves, tails on bones with springs (standard in Genshin Impact and
  anime games); cheap and very alive.
- **The face is driven by the voice** (NVIDIA Audio2Face: lips, cheeks, brows from the sound). In moood every line is
  voiced, and the face is silent.
- **Staged for the camera.** In cut-scenes poses and motion are made for a particular shot: what isn't seen doesn't
  matter, what is seen is weighed.
