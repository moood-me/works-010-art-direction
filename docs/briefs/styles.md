# Brief for every agent of "Стили персонажей"

## Why
Repository `/home/user/works-010-art-direction` (read its `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/README.md`, `docs/problem.md`,
`docs/references.md` and look at the pictures in `docs/pictures/references/`) is about beautiful stylised characters
for moood's games. Pavel (the owner) says our characters so far look like invalids, or from ugly cartoons, or drawn by
unskilled artists; in beautiful works you see soul, mood, style and fine details that give a very pleasant feeling —
the characters are aesthetically great, you want to meet them, they look like attractive people, even when drawn
simply or symbolically. He has no artist; he wants us (Claude) to do it, and believes we can: we have taste in design
and eyes to tell attractive from unattractive.

The approach agreed: don't invent a style — take it from real works that are beautiful. Study how the style is built
(proportions, how eye, nose, mouth are drawn, shapes and lines, light and colour), write it down as the style's rules,
and draw our own characters by those rules — many variants, compared by eye side by side with the samples. We take
the style's language, not their characters: never redraw their characters.

## Your job (one style, named in your task)
1. **Find beautiful samples of the style** — character art from the works named in your task and others of the same
   style (stills, model sheets, turnarounds, expression sheets, concept art). Search the web, download, look at them
   with your own eyes; keep 6–12 good ones as small copies in `refs/` (≤ 800 px, jpg/webp; third-party, kept only
   as references; list sources and links in `notes.md`). Choose beautiful, not merely typical.
2. **Write the style's rules** — concrete enough that another agent could draw by them: head and body proportions
   (heads tall, where the eyes sit, face to cranium), the eye (shape, lids, iris, highlight, lashes), nose, mouth, ears,
   brows, hair (masses, locks, tips), hands, the line (weight, taper, where it is), shapes (shape language, straight
   against curve), colour (palette, value, how skin is coloured), light and shading (flat, cel, soft, painted), how
   men/women/children/old people differ, how expressions are drawn. Each rule is something you saw in the samples.
3. **Draw the cast in SVG by those rules** — the same cast in every style, so styles can be compared:
   - **Аня**, about 20 — warm, trusting, dreamy;
   - **Савелий Петрович**, about 70, a watchmaker — grumpy outside, kind inside;
   - **Тим**, about 8 — mischievous, curious;
   - **Вера**, about 40 — strict, clever, tired.
   For each: a head-and-shoulders portrait, the full figure; for at least two of them a turnaround (front,
   three-quarter, side); for at least one an expression sheet (joy, surprise, sadness, anger). Plus one picture of the
   four together. Original characters — not copies of anyone.
4. **Iterate by eye.** Render your SVGs to PNG (headless Chromium + Playwright, or a converter), put each next to the
   samples, look as a person would: is it attractive? does it look like this style? would Pavel want to meet them?
   Redraw what is weak; try several variants of a face and keep the best. Don't show what you yourself find ugly or
   off-style — if something stays weak after honest tries, show it and say so.

## The page
Your folder `styles/<folder>/` (format in `styles/README.md`). Blocks, in Russian, plain and short: «Образцы» (refs
with captions), «Правила стиля» (the rules as a list, maybe with small diagram pictures), then the cast — portraits,
figures, turnarounds, expressions, together. `cover`: the four together. `verdict`: honest — how close to the style,
how attractive, what is weak, what SVG can't do here. Keep the folder under ~15 MB.
