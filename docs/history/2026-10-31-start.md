# 2026-10-31 — the start

**What called for it.** Pavel compared the published moood games with the scenes Claude Design made for "hope" and
found the games far below them — scattered, assembled at random, people that move and look frightening — while
works-009-hope showed engine 3 can draw those scenes almost one to one. The question became: how to make beautiful
games on engine 3, and first of all, how to make people beautiful.

**What was done.**
- Looked at the renders of published games (Canterville, Usher, Paradiso, The Lady with the Dog) through the studio's
  kept pictures, without new renders; read moood's docs on the engine, people and motion.
- Took apart why the hope scenes pull you in, and what an ideal mid-shot would be ([scenes.md](../scenes.md)).
- Built a 2D mid-shot dialogue, then a code-sculpted 3D body with cloth; both showed that bodies made from formulas
  don't become human. Took Anny from moood instead.
- Built the page of body styles on Anny: genres, characters, shape language, the silhouette as one form, the sign.
  Asked how many bodies were beautiful — none — and why ([people.md](../people.md), seven reasons). Pavel chose to work
  2, 3 and 5 deeply on one body across style vectors.
- Moved the style to a difference between a styled and a neutral construction applied to the real body; made
  simplification of small forms an even smoothing in centimetres.
- Decided with Pavel to try two directions at once: the designed form as primary, and dressed people with a good
  head.
- Made this repository (Pavel created it; the title "Art direction").

**How, to repeat it.** The pages are in [experiments/](../../experiments/README.md). Anny's files come from moood's
`engines/1/bodies/` and are turned into base64 JS modules so a published page can load them. Pictures were looked at
with headless Chromium (Playwright with SwiftShader) through `one.js` / `rr.js` and tiled with `sheet.py`.
