## Cartoon, yet anatomical

`cartoon-7.png` … `cartoon-10.png`, `cartoon-anatomical-1.png` (Big Hero 6, Rise of the Guardians, Spider-Verse, Star
vs. the Forces of Evil), `cartoon-anatomical-2.png` (Ariel fan art), `cartoon-anatomical-3.png` (a 3D cartoon boy),
`toy-doll-3d.png` (a 3D doll-like girl with purple hair).

Drawn parts, yet they look anatomical. What holds it:
- **The face is built around the eyes,** not eyes put into a face: a big cranium, a small face set low, a tiny simple
  nose, a small mouth, cheeks and chin as one soft form.
- **The eye is part of the skin:** an almond opening shaped by the lids, the lids continuing the face around the
  eyeball, a big iris, a glint, lashes as a form along the lid edge.
- **Drawn features lie on a soft 3D form:** brows, lash line, lips, iris are crisp graphic shapes painted on the skin,
  while the form stays anatomical.
- **Hair is designed locks:** big ribbons with sharp tips, a volume larger than the skull, framing the face; the
  hairstyle's silhouette reads on its own.
- **Anatomy stays human, proportions shift together:** skeleton, joints, neck, shoulders, hands with fingers are real;
  what is cartoon moves as one — a bigger head goes with bigger eyes, a smaller nose, rounder cheeks.
- **A living face:** one brow up, a smirk, a tilted head — expression shapes for brows, lids and mouth corners.

## Indie heroes

`indie-roster-chibi.png`, `indie-roster-icons.png`.

Two or three heads tall; a body of a few simple forms, not anatomy; known by silhouette and one main thing (a mask,
horns, a helmet, a hat); a face often two dots. What it takes:
- a constructor of characters from simple forms (balls, drops, cylinders) with smooth joins — a head of half the
  height and short legs can't come from a human body;
- a skeleton for such builds, so Kimodo still drives them (motion carried onto very short limbs);
- things as part of the silhouette: masks, helmets, horns, hats, weapons;
- non-human heroes: animals, robots, ghosts.

## Expressive faces and bends

`expressive-faces.webp` (Hazbin Hotel, The Amazing Digital Circus and others).

- the face as a graphic layer that changes a lot: a huge mouth with teeth, eyes of any shape, a wide grin;
- rubber-hose limbs that bend in an arc rather than break at a joint — limbs along a curve;
- shape language at its limit: sharp triangles, horns, tails.

## Alive: flowing hair, moving faces

`anime-hair-closeup.png`, `kpop-demon-hunters.png`, `cel-action-group.webp`.

Aliveness is layers, each made its own way, all at once:
- **hair as locks,** each a ribbon on a chain of bones, moved by physics — heavy, stiff at the root and soft to the tip,
  tips lagging the head, swinging and settling, colliding with head and shoulders, uneven wind; drawn strand lines and
  highlights on top (rendering, put aside for now);
- **a set of face shapes** (brows, lids, cheeks, mouth corners, jaw — commonly ARKit's 52), drawn for the style;
- **mouth and cheeks from the voice** (Audio2Face, now open) — moood's lines are voiced;
- **eyes that live by themselves:** blinks at uneven intervals and on head turns, small saccades, the gaze turning
  before the head;
- **drawn features that move with the face** — brows and mouth line as graphics on the skin that change shape too;
- **breath** always, and **cloth** by physics like hair; body motion from Kimodo.
Natural because nothing repeats in a visible cycle and hair and cloth answer any motion by physics, not keyframes.

## A turnaround sheet of a cartoon hero

(A sheet Pavel showed — "Jam", a creature superhero, front / three-quarter / back with a palette and a note "anxious
but determined"; not saved, it came only inline.) "Imagine it is a person drawn in this style — to have this in 3D."
- a build from simple forms (a head a third of the height with tufts, a small body, thin legs, huge boots);
- fitting forms to the sheet's three views at once, or a picture-to-3D model as a base;
- a graphic face: huge eyes in a mask, a wide grin, expression carrying the character;
- spring bones for tufts, tail, cape;
- a human-scheme skeleton with short legs and big feet, feet pinned to the floor after retargeting;
- flat colour zones from the palette and an outline — without them such a character in 3D looks like a plastic toy.
